**Why it matters** (para 2-13): a clean body is less likely to get sick. Changing dirty socks often protects your feet, and unwashed clothes and hair can lead to lice.

**Wash or sanitize your hands** (paras 2-16, 2-18, 2-19) — one of the most effective ways to stop disease. At a minimum:
- before and after eating or snacking, and before handling food
- after using the latrine
- after handling anything that could carry germs, and often during the day
Use soap and drinkable (potable) water. When you don't have soap and water, use alcohol-based hand sanitizer — but it doesn't work on hands caked with dirt or grease.

**Don't share toiletries** (para 2-15) — it spreads disease and infection.

**Bathing** (para 2-20): ideally a shower every day, or at least once a week. With no shower, wash daily with a washcloth, paying attention to sweaty or wet areas — armpits, feet, groin, between the thighs and buttocks, and under the breasts.

**Keep your skin dry** (para 2-24): foot powder on your feet; absorbent body powder where wetness is a problem. Skip perfume, cologne and scented soap in the field — they attract insects.

**Feet** (para 2-47): bring several pairs of issue boot socks and keep them clean and dry; change wet or damp socks as soon as possible; wash your feet daily if you can; put foot powder on your feet and in your boots when you change socks.

**Latrines** (paras 2-69, 2-70):
- **100 meters or more** from food operations — downwind and downslope if possible.
- **30 meters or more** downslope from wells, springs, streams and other water sources.
- Handwashing at the latrine is mandatory.

**Water in hot weather** (paras 2-81, 2-84): up to **three gallons a day** per Soldier just for drinking. Drink small amounts often, and drink before hard work. Only plain water goes in your canteen — add flavoring only in your canteen cup. Use the buddy system.

## Quiz
Q: What is one of the most effective ways to protect yourself from disease in the field? | A: Washing or sanitizing your hands frequently
Q: Name three times you must wash or sanitize your hands. | A: Any three: before eating, after eating, before handling food, after using the latrine, after handling anything that could carry germs
Q: When does alcohol-based hand sanitizer not work well? | A: When your hands are caked with dirt or grease
Q: Why shouldn't you share toiletries? | A: It can spread disease and infection
Q: If there are no showers, how should you clean yourself? | A: Wash daily with a washcloth, especially sweaty or wet areas
Q: How far must a field latrine be from food operations? | A: 100 meters or more, downwind and downslope if possible
Q: How far must a field latrine be from a water source? | A: 30 meters or more, downslope
Q: What should you do with wet or damp socks? | A: Change them as soon as possible
Q: How much drinking water may a Soldier need per day in hot weather? | A: Up to three gallons
Q: Can you put flavoring in your canteen? | A: No — only plain water in the canteen; use the canteen cup for flavored water
Q: Why avoid scented soap and cologne in the field? | A: They can attract insects
