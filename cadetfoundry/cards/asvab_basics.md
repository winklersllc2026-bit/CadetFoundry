The **ASVAB** (Armed Services Vocational Aptitude Battery) is the test every service uses. Its scores are used to decide **who can enlist**, to **match people to military jobs**, and to help students **explore careers**.

**The subtests** (in the order they're given): General Science, **Arithmetic Reasoning**, **Word Knowledge**, **Paragraph Comprehension**, **Mathematics Knowledge**, Electronics Information, Auto Information, Shop Information, Mechanical Comprehension and Assembling Objects.

**The AFQT** (Armed Forces Qualification Test) score is built from four of them: **Arithmetic Reasoning, Mathematics Knowledge, Paragraph Comprehension and Word Knowledge**. It's reported as a **percentile from 1 to 99** — an AFQT of 50 means you scored as well as or better than 50% of a national sample of 18–23 year olds.

**On the computer test (CAT-ASVAB)** the questions adapt to how you're doing, so it can be shorter than the paper test. It takes about 2 hours. **No calculator** is allowed.

**At school:** the ASVAB is also given to high school students through the **ASVAB Career Exploration Program**, which is free for participating schools. Eligible scores from it can be used to enlist (a verification test may be required).

**How to prepare** (the official advice): take solid math, English and science classes, and use the free practice at officialasvab.com, asvabprogram.com and march2success.com. Real test questions are kept secret, and anyone claiming to sell them or guarantee a score is not legitimate.
