The Navy's **Physical Fitness Assessment (PFA)** includes **body composition** and the **Physical Readiness Test (PRT)**.

**The PRT, in order** (Guide-5A):
1. **Push-ups** — as many as possible with correct form in **two minutes**. Push-ups done incorrectly don't count.
2. **Forearm plank** — hold a proper forearm plank as long as you can, up to the maximum time.
3. **1.5-mile run or walk** — the standard cardio test.

**Alternate cardio:** commanding officers may allow a **stationary bike, treadmill, rowing machine (Concept2) or swim** instead of the run.

**What it measures:** cardiorespiratory fitness (the run) and **muscular endurance** — the plank for the abdominal core, push-ups for the upper body.

**At boot camp:** recruits get a check in **week 4** (planks, push-ups and the 1.5-mile run) and take the **official PFA in week 8**. They must pass it to graduate.

The rules are in OPNAVINST 6110.1L and the Navy's Physical Readiness Program guides, and scoring tables change, so check the current guide for numbers.

Q: Name an alternate cardio option on the Navy PRT. | A: The stationary bike | WRONG: A 3-mile ruck march; Jumping rope; A 100-meter sprint
Q: In which week of boot camp do Navy recruits take the official PFA? | A: Week 8
Q: What does PRT stand for? | A: Physical Readiness Test | WRONG: Personal Running Time; Physical Recruit Training; Push-up and Run Test
