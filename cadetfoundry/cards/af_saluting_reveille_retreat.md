**The hand salute** (para 3.5): fingers extended and joined, palm flat and tilted slightly toward the face, upper arm parallel to the ground. The **tip of the middle finger touches the right front corner of the headdress** (or the outside corner of the right eyebrow with a non-billed hat).

**Exchanging salutes** (para 3.6) — "the **junior member always saluting first**":
- **Outdoors**, salutes are exchanged on recognition between officers and between officers and enlisted members or cadets. Salute early enough for the officer to return it.
- **Indoors**, salutes aren't given except for **formal reporting** and formal presentations such as awards.
- **In formation**, don't salute unless commanded; the person in charge salutes for the group.
- **In a group, not in formation**, the first person to notice a senior officer calls the group to attention, and everyone salutes.
- No saluting in "No Salute" areas such as flight lines.

**Reveille** (para 7.23) signals the **start of the official duty day**, often with the flag raising. On the first note of the national anthem or "To the Colors," the flag is raised briskly, and the senior member keeps it from touching the ground (para 7.24).

**Retreat** (para 7.26) signals the **end of the duty day** and pays respect to the flag. Outdoors in uniform and not in a formation, you face the flag (or the music) and stand at parade rest, then come to attention and salute for the anthem.

The flag is always raised and lowered from the **leeward (downwind) side** of the flagstaff (para 7.25.2).

Q: On an Air Force salute, what touches the headdress? | A: The tip of the middle finger | WRONG: The palm of the hand; The tip of the index finger; The thumb
Q: Name a time you do salute indoors. | A: Formal reporting | WRONG: Entering the dining facility; Passing an officer in a hallway; Walking into a classroom
Q: What does reveille signal? | A: The start of the official duty day | WRONG: The end of the official duty day; Lights out; The start of lunch
Q: What does retreat signal? | A: The end of the official duty day | WRONG: The start of the official duty day; Lights out; An emergency formation
