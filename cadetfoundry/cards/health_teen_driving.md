Driving is one of the biggest risks a teenager takes. The CDC names the main risk factors:

- **Inexperience** — teens are more likely to underestimate or not recognize dangerous situations.
- **Night and weekend driving** — teen drivers' fatal crash rate at night is about **3 times** that of adults. In 2020, **44%** of teen crash deaths happened between **9 p.m. and 6 a.m.**, and **half** happened on a **Friday, Saturday or Sunday**.
- **No seat belt** — in 2020, **56%** of teen drivers and passengers killed in crashes **weren't wearing a seat belt**.
- **Distraction** — in 2019, **39%** of high school drivers **texted or emailed while driving** at least once in a month.
- **Speeding** — teens are more likely than older drivers to speed.
- **Alcohol** — **any amount** of alcohol before driving increases a teen's crash risk.
- **Drugs** — driving impaired by any substance is dangerous and **illegal**; after alcohol, marijuana is the most common.
- **Teen passengers** — having teen or young adult passengers **increases the crash risk** of a teen driver.

**Your rules:** buckle up every ride, **phone away**, follow the speed limit, never drive impaired — and never ride with a driver who is. Call a parent or trusted adult for a ride instead.
