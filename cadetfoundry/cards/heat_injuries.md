**Heat stroke is a medical emergency.** Get a medic or call 911 **now**, and tell your drill sergeant or leader. Treatment must begin immediately to prevent permanent damage or death (ATP 4-25.12, para 7-10).

**Signs of heat stroke** (FM 7-22): no sweating, hot dry skin, rapid pulse, rapid breathing, dizziness or confusion, unconsciousness, seizure, or coma.
- Early signs can be headache, dizziness, confusion, weakness, nausea, vomiting and feeling very hot (ATP 4-25.12, para 7-10).
- **Don't wait for dry skin:** some heat stroke casualties are still wet with sweat, so skin alone can't tell you how bad it is (para 7-10).
- It can come on **suddenly**, with collapse and passing out (para 7-10).
- In exertional heat stroke the core body temperature is **above 104 °F** (ATP 4-25.12, para 11-6).

**Signs of heat exhaustion** (FM 7-22) — **needs medical attention:** excessive thirst, fatigue, lack of coordination, heavy sweating, cool or wet skin, dizziness, confusion. Heat exhaustion is the most common heat injury (ATP 4-25.12, para 7-9).

**Vomiting over and over** after heat exposure: that Soldier should be **evacuated immediately** (ATP 4-25.12, para 7-16).

**Too much water is dangerous too.** Drinking far too much (more than 12 quarts a day) without eating salted food can cause hyponatremia, which can kill and looks like a heat injury (ATP 4-25.12, para 7-16).

A Soldier who has had heat stroke once stays at **higher risk** of another heat injury (para 7-10).
