**What JROTC is:** the Junior Reserve Officers' Training Corps is a federally funded program of instruction for high school students, run by the Armed Forces.

**Its purpose, in federal law** (10 U.S.C. § 2031): "to instill in students in United States secondary educational institutions the values of **citizenship, service to the United States** (including an introduction to service opportunities in military, national, and public service), and **personal responsibility and a sense of accomplishment**."

**History**
- **1916** — Congress created JROTC as an **Army** program in the **National Defense Act of 1916**.
- **1964** — expanded to the **Navy and Air Force**. Marine Corps, Space Force and Coast Guard units followed.

**Today:** about **3,500 units** in all 50 states and overseas. **Army JROTC** is the largest — about **1,744 units and 285,000 Cadets**.

**What Cadets study:** over three or four years — **leadership, ethics, civics, history, and health and wellness**, plus activities such as **color guard, drill team, marksmanship** and field trips.

**Your instructors** are usually **retired officers and noncommissioned officers (NCOs)**. Schools generally have at least one officer (the **Senior Military Instructor**) and one NCO (the **Junior Military Instructor**).

**No obligation:** joining JROTC **does not create any military obligation**, and recruiting is **not a stated objective** in law or policy.
