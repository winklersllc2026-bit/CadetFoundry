**RPE — rating of perceived exertion** — describes **how hard a workout feels** on a **1 to 10 scale**. Using it helps you dose your effort and **avoid overtraining and injury** (para 6-27).

**The scale** (table 6-3, simplified):
- **1** — no effort: sitting, lying down, stretching
- **3** — very easy: walking; you could keep it up all day
- **4** — easy: jogging slowly; you could keep it up for hours
- **5** — moderate: breathing heavily as it gets harder
- **6** — somewhat hard: you're feeling it
- **7** — hard: short of breath, **speaking in short sentences**
- **8** — very hard: pushing your limits; **can't keep this pace**
- **9** — very, very hard: **can barely breathe to speak**
- **10** — maximum: can't do any more

**RIR — repetitions in reserve** — for strength training: **how many more reps you could do before you'd have to stop.** Easier sets leave more reps in reserve; a true one-rep maximum has **zero** in reserve.

**Use it:** a long easy run should feel around **4–5**. If every workout feels like a 9, you're likely overdoing it. Knowing the target effort in advance helps you prepare mentally.
