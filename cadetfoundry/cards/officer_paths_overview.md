Every service has a few ways in. **Almost every path needs a bachelor's degree by the day you're commissioned.**

**1. A service academy** — four years of college with the military built in, tuition covered.
- **West Point** (Army), the **Naval Academy** (Navy and Marine Corps), the **Air Force Academy** (Air Force and Space Force).
- West Point and the Naval Academy need a **nomination**, usually from a **member of Congress**.

**2. ROTC in college** — military classes and training while you go to a regular college, often with a scholarship.
- **Army ROTC**, **Navy ROTC** (with a **Marine option**) and **Air Force ROTC** (for the Air Force or Space Force).

**3. Officer candidate school, for college graduates**
- Army **OCS** — 12 weeks. Navy **OCS** — 13 weeks in Newport, Rhode Island. Marine Corps **Officer Candidate Course** — 10 weeks at Quantico, Virginia.

**4. Enlisted to officer** — e.g. the Army's **Green to Gold** (enlisted Soldiers go to college through ROTC) and the Navy's **Limited Duty Officer** program.

**5. Direct commission** — for professionals such as **lawyers, doctors and chaplains**.

New officers start as **Second Lieutenants** — or **Ensigns** in the Navy.

Requirements change — check the official site of the service, or a recruiter, before you plan.
