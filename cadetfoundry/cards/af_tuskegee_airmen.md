During World War II the U.S. military was **segregated**. The Army Air Forces ran an experiment at **Tuskegee, Alabama**. It proved that African Americans, given equal training and opportunity, could fly in, command and support combat units as well as anyone.

**The 99th Fighter Squadron:**
- Tuskegee graduated its **first five fighter pilots on March 7, 1942**.
- **Capt. Benjamin O. Davis Jr.**, a West Point graduate, led the squadron overseas. He later became the Air Force's **first African American general**. His father was the Army's first African American general.
- The 99th first saw combat in **June 1943**, flying P-40 Warhawks over the Mediterranean.
- When critics questioned the squadron, Davis defended it. The 99th flew more missions per pilot than other units and "never ran from a fight."

**The 332nd Fighter Group, the "Red Tails":**
- Davis came home to command the **332nd Fighter Group**. It flew **P-51 Mustangs** and painted their tails **bright red**.
- The group flew **311 missions** from June 1944 to the end of the war. **179** of them escorted bombers.
- On **March 24, 1945**, they escorted B-17s all the way to **Berlin** and shot down three German **jet** fighters. The group earned a **Distinguished Unit Citation**.

**The record:**
- About **990 pilots** trained at Tuskegee, and **350** went overseas.
- They shot down **112** enemy aircraft.
- About **150** Tuskegee Airmen died, and 32 became prisoners of war.

Their success helped lead to the **integration** of the armed forces, "with the U.S. Air Force leading the way."
