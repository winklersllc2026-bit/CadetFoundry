Following the Army's customs, courtesies and traditions **connects you to Soldiers throughout America's history** (para 1-18).

- **Custom** — an **established practice**: things you do, and things you avoid (taboos).
- **Courtesy** — **good manners and politeness** in dealing with others. Military courtesy is **vital to maintaining discipline**.
- **Tradition** — beliefs and customs **handed down from one generation to the next** by word of mouth or example. Military traditions are the **"Army Way"** of doing and thinking. **Army traditions** are things everyone in the Army does everywhere; **unit traditions** belong to one unit.
- **Pride** — the commitment to master the knowledge and skills of a professional.

**Why we drill** (para 1-19): to move a unit from place to place **in an orderly manner**, to build **habits of precision and response to the leader's orders**, and to give everyone **practice commanding troops**.

**Ceremonies** (para 1-20) show the **pride, discipline and teamwork** of a unit, build esprit de corps, and preserve tradition. Sometimes a ceremony is **the only contact the public has with the Army.**
- **Review** — first described in von Steuben's Blue Book: troops form, honors are presented, the troops are inspected, and they **march in review**.
- **Parade** — once the daily formation for guard duty; now a way to inspect troops, present awards and share information.
- **Reveille and Retreat** — honor the daily **raising** and **lowering** of the flag.
