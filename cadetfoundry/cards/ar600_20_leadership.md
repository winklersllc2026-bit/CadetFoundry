**Chain of command** (para 2-1): helps commanders accomplish the unit's mission while caring for the people and property in their charge. A simple, direct chain passes orders from the highest to the lowest levels in the least time and with the least chance of misinterpretation. **Commanders are responsible for everything their command does or fails to do.** They delegate authority, but keep overall responsibility. **Soldiers will use the chain of command** when communicating issues and problems to their leaders and commanders.

**NCO support channel** (para 2-19): the "leadership chain." It **parallels and complements the chain of command** — a channel of communication and supervision **from the CSM to the 1SG, and then to other NCOs and enlisted Soldiers**. It helps the chain of command with, among other things:
- training enlisted Soldiers in their MOS and in basic Soldier skills;
- supervising physical fitness training and the weight and appearance standards (AR 600-9, AR 670-1);
- teaching Army history, customs, courtesies and traditions;
- caring for Soldiers and their Families, on and off duty;
- accounting for and maintaining Soldiers' arms and equipment.

**Open-door policy** (para 2-2): every commander publishes one. It lets Soldiers bring facts, concerns and problems — personal or professional — that they haven't been able to resolve. Soldiers are responsible for making sure the commander knows about problems that affect discipline, morale and mission effectiveness.

**Military discipline** (para 4-1): founded on **self-discipline, respect for properly constituted authority, and embracing the professional Army Ethic**. It shows in teamwork, smart appearance, well-kept equipment and quarters, respect between seniors and subordinates, prompt and willing execution of legal orders, and fairness for all Soldiers.
