In an emergency, the people nearby are the first help. FEMA's **five actions**:

**1. Call 9-1-1.** **Always** make the call — even if you think someone else already did. 911 operators are highly trained: they'll tell you what to do and help you stay focused. It's normal to feel stressed; **take a deep breath and lean on the operator.**

**2. Stay safe.** You can't help anyone if you get hurt. Pause and use your senses — **what do you see, hear and smell?** (Traffic, fire, fumes.) Move someone out of immediate danger only if it's safe to do so.

**3. Stop the bleeding.** **Bleeding is a fast killer** — a person can lose a deadly amount of blood within minutes.
- Press **firmly and directly** on the wound with a clean cloth, bandage or even clothing, and **keep pressing** until the bleeding stops or help takes over (MedlinePlus).
- If blood soaks through, **don't remove it — add another layer on top.**
- A **tourniquet** is for life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg when pressure isn't enough — placed 2–3 inches above the wound, not on a joint. Note the time you put it on.

**4. Position the injured.**
- **Awake?** Let them sit or lie however they feel best. Don't force them.
- **Unconscious but breathing?** Put them in the **recovery position** — on their side, to keep the airway open and prevent choking.
- **Not breathing?** Start **CPR** (see the CPR card).

**5. Provide comfort.** Kind, calm words can matter as much as medical help. Say your name, ask **"How can I help?"**, keep them calm, and tell them **help is coming**.

Learn hands-on skills in a certified first aid and CPR course.
