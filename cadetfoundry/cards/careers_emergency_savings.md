**Savings** go in safe places where you can get your money any time — **savings accounts, checking accounts and certificates of deposit (CDs)**. Your money may be insured by the **FDIC** (banks) or the **NCUA** (credit unions).

**The tradeoff:** savings are safe and available, but they **earn a low interest rate.**

**An emergency fund:** most smart savers keep enough in savings to cover an emergency, like suddenly losing a job. Some keep **up to six months of their income** in savings so it will absolutely be there when they need it.

**Inflation:** if your savings don't earn enough to keep up with rising prices, your money buys less over time. Save a dollar when it buys a loaf of bread, and years later that dollar plus interest might only buy **half a loaf**. That's why many people keep **some** money in savings for emergencies and **invest** some to earn more over longer periods.

**Start small:** put part of every paycheck into savings first — even a little — and list savings as a line in your budget.
