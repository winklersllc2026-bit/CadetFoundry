A credit card is a **loan**. Whatever you don't pay back on time costs you **interest**.

**Key terms** (CFPB):
- **Interest rate** — the price you pay for borrowing money.
- **APR (annual percentage rate)** — the standard way to compare how much loans cost.
- **Grace period** — the time between the end of a billing cycle and your payment due date.

**How interest works:** many card companies charge interest **daily**, based on your average daily balance. **The sooner you pay off all or some of your balance, the less interest you pay.**

**Avoid interest completely:** if your card has a grace period, **pay the full balance by the due date** every month and you avoid interest on purchases.

**Habits from the FTC:**
- **Pay in full** if you can.
- **Pay on time** — by the due date, every month.
- **Paying only the minimum makes credit more expensive** — you pay interest on everything you didn't pay back.
- **Review your statements** for charges you don't recognize.
- Federal law limits what you owe for **unauthorized charges to $50**.

Paying on time, every time, also builds a good credit report.
