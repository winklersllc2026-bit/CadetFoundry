**Who can be an Army officer** (goarmy.com):
- A **college graduate** by the time you're commissioned.
- A **U.S. citizen** by the time you're commissioned.
- **At least 17 but under 31** in the year you're commissioned — **under 27** if you commission from West Point.

**The paths:**
- **Army ROTC** — college classes and training on campus, with scholarships available. You become an officer after **four years of college** and start as a **second lieutenant**.
- **West Point** (the U.S. Military Academy) — a funded college education plus an annual stipend of **$10,000+**. You need a **nomination** — usually from a U.S. Representative or Senator. Applicants must be **at least 17 and not yet 23 on July 1** of the year they enter, **not married**, and with **no legal duty to support a child**. Graduates serve **at least five years on active duty**.
- **Officer Candidate School (OCS)** — a **12-week** course for enlisted Soldiers and civilians with college degrees. Graduates are commissioned as **second lieutenants**.
- **Green to Gold** — for **enlisted Soldiers** to finish a degree and earn a commission.
- **Direct commission** — for civilians with professional experience, such as in **law, medicine or religion**.

Requirements change — confirm them on goarmy.com or with a recruiter.
