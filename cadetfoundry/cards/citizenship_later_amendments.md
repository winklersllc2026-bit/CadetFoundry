After the Bill of Rights, later amendments widened who is free, who is a citizen, and who can vote.

- **13th Amendment** (ratified December 6, 1865) — ended slavery: "Neither slavery nor involuntary servitude… shall exist within the United States."
- **14th Amendment** (ratified July 9, 1868) — everyone born or naturalized in the United States (and subject to its jurisdiction) is a **citizen**, and every person gets the **equal protection of the laws**.
- **15th Amendment** (ratified February 3, 1870) — the right of citizens to vote can't be denied or abridged on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude.
- **19th Amendment** (ratified August 18, 1920) — the right to vote can't be denied **on account of sex**: women won the vote nationwide.
- **26th Amendment** (ratified July 1, 1971) — citizens **18 years of age or older** can vote.

The Constitution has **27 amendments** in all.
