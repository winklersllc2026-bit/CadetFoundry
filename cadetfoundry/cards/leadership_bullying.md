**Bullying** is unwanted, aggressive behavior that involves a **real or perceived power imbalance** and is **repeated, or likely to be repeated**, over time.

**Two parts make it bullying:**
- **An imbalance of power** — physical strength, embarrassing information, or popularity used to control or harm someone.
- **Repetition** — it happens more than once, or could.

**Three types:**
- **Verbal** — saying or writing mean things: teasing, name-calling, taunting, threats.
- **Social** (relational) — hurting someone's reputation or relationships: leaving someone out on purpose, telling others not to be friends with them, spreading rumors, embarrassing them in public.
- **Physical** — hurting someone's body or things: hitting, tripping, pushing, taking or breaking their stuff, rude gestures.

About **1 in 5** students ages 12–18 reported being bullied (2022 federal survey data).

**If it's happening to you:** **speak up** — tell a trusted adult. It's better to let an adult know than to let the problem continue. Stay calm and respectful.

**If you hurt someone and regret it:** talk to them privately, **apologize**, admit what you did hurt them, and ask them to help you understand how it made them feel.

**If friends want to give someone the "silent treatment":** don't take part in public or online shaming. You can say, "I disagree with them, but I don't want to bully anyone."
