A **reference** is someone who can tell an employer about your work history, skills and work style. What they say can make a big difference.

**Four types of references:**
- **Employment** — past employers, co-workers or clients. These carry **the most weight**. Odd jobs count too: babysitting, lawn mowing, volunteer work.
- **Professional** — people who know you through clubs, businesses or community organizations.
- **Academic** — **instructors** and counselors. Best for current students and recent graduates — like your JROTC instructor.
- **Personal** — people who know you personally. Use them only if you don't have the other types.

**Choosing well:**
- Pick people who honestly know you and will speak objectively.
- **Avoid family members and close friends.**

**Before you list someone:**
- **Ask first** if they're willing to be your reference.
- Get permission to share their contact information and ask how they prefer to be contacted.
- Tell them about the job — many like to see the job description and your resume.
- **Thank them** when they've given you a recommendation.
