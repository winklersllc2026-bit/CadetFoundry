The Space Force runs the nation's two main launch ranges, through its **Space Launch Deltas**. They support launches for the military, **NASA** and **commercial companies**. Launching is part of the **space access** core function: getting payloads to space safely.

**East Coast: Space Launch Delta 45**
- Based at **Patrick Space Force Base** and **Cape Canaveral Space Force Station**, Florida.
- Runs the **Eastern Range**, which covers **15 million square miles**.
- Launches Atlas V and Falcon 9 rockets, carrying satellites such as **GPS** and missile-warning satellites.
- Its vision: "The World's Premier Gateway to Space."

**West Coast: Space Launch Delta 30**
- Based at **Vandenberg Space Force Base**, California. Vandenberg began in 1941 as an Army camp and joined the Air Force in 1957.
- Its remote coastline lets rockets launch into **polar orbit** (an orbit over the poles) **without flying over populated areas**. It is also used to test intercontinental ballistic missiles.
- In **1958**, Vandenberg launched **Discoverer 1**, the world's first polar-orbiting satellite.
- More than 99,000 acres, with 42 miles of protected coastline.

**Range safety.** Guardians make sure the area around a launch is safe for takeoff and protect the launched spacecraft from collision. It's a big job: the Vandenberg fact sheet listed about **7,000 range operations a year**.
