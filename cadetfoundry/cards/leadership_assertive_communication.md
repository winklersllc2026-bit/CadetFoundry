**Conflict is normal.** Self-awareness, self-confidence, **assertiveness** and **conflict resolution** skills let you work through everyday people problems (para 9-46).

**Assertive communication is objective, specific and empathetic** (paras 9-47, 9-50):
- **Objective** — focus on **the task, not the person**. "The report was late" — not "You're lazy." When a conflict turns personal, it damages the team.
- **Specific** — focus on **one or two** specific things to fix, not a list of everything wrong.
- **Empathetic** — remember everyone involved has their own **point of view, needs and goals**. Understand the situation from their side and respond to their concerns.

**Communication is a loop** (para 9-48): one person sends a message, the other receives it — sometimes differently than intended — and gives **feedback**. **Barriers** get in the way: noise and surroundings, **emotions** on either side, culture, experience, and the **channel** (face to face vs. a text).

**Most communication is nonverbal** (para 9-49) — body language and facial expressions. **Tone** matters too: the same words can mean different things depending on pitch, speed and emphasis.

**Teams that communicate well hold together** (para 9-52): **task cohesion** comes from working toward common goals; **social cohesion** comes from **mutual respect**.

**Try it:** "When the gear was left out after practice (*specific, objective*), I had to stay late to put it away. I know you had a ride waiting (*empathy*). Can we split cleanup next time?"

## Quiz
Q: What three qualities make communication assertive? | A: Objective, specific and empathetic | WRONG: Loud, fast and forceful; Vague, general and polite; Angry, personal and blunt
Q: What does objective communication focus on? | A: The task, not the person | WRONG: The person, not the task; Your feelings about the person; Who is to blame
Q: How many things should assertive feedback focus on? | A: One or two specific things | WRONG: Everything they did wrong; Ten things at once; Nothing specific
Q: What is empathy? | A: Understanding the situation from another person's point of view | WRONG: Feeling sorry for someone; Agreeing with everything someone says; Doing someone's work for them
Q: Is most communication verbal or nonverbal? | A: Nonverbal
Q: Name two barriers to communication | A: Physical surroundings, environment, emotions, culture, experience, the communication channel | WRONG: Clear words, eye contact, active listening; A quiet room, a calm voice, good timing; Asking questions, taking notes, summarizing
Q: What is social cohesion? | A: The bond team members have from mutual respect | WRONG: Following orders without question; Being friends only with people like you; Doing tasks alone
Q: Why does tone matter? | A: The same words can mean different things depending on pitch, speed and emphasis | WRONG: It doesn't — only the words matter; Louder always means more respect; Tone only matters in writing
