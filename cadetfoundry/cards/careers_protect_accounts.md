Your phone, email and social media accounts hold your money, your messages and your reputation. The FTC's advice:

**1. Keep software up to date.** Update your phone, tablet and computer as soon as possible, and **turn on automatic updates**.

**2. Use strong passwords.**
- Aim for **at least 15 characters**.
- Mix uppercase and lowercase letters, numbers and symbols — or use a **passphrase of random words** (not song lyrics, movie quotes or common phrases).
- A password manager can create strong passwords — protect it with a strong password of its own.

**3. Turn on two-factor authentication.** It adds an extra layer of security: after your password, you also need a **code** (by text or email), an **authenticator app**, or a **security key**. The app or security key is the more secure option.

**4. Watch for phishing.** **Don't click a link in an unexpected email or text.** Contact the company using a phone number or website you know is real.

**5. If something goes wrong, act quickly.** If someone uses your identity, report it at **IdentityTheft.gov** for a recovery plan — and tell a parent or trusted adult.
