**The Emancipation Proclamation** — issued by **President Abraham Lincoln** on **January 1, 1863**, during the Civil War.
- It declared that all persons held as slaves in the states in rebellion "**are, and henceforward shall be free.**"
- **Its limits:** it applied only to the Confederate states in rebellion. It did **not** free people held in slavery in the loyal border states or areas already under Union control, and its promise depended on a Union victory.
- It announced that **Black men would be accepted into the Union Army and Navy**. By the end of the war, **almost 200,000** Black soldiers and sailors had served.
- Slavery was ended nationwide by the **13th Amendment**, ratified **December 6, 1865**.

**The Gettysburg Address** — Lincoln's short speech dedicating the national cemetery at Gettysburg, **November 19, 1863**. It begins:

> Fourscore and seven years ago our fathers brought forth upon this continent a new nation, conceived in liberty, and dedicated to the proposition that all men are created equal.

"Fourscore and seven" means **87** — counting back from 1863 to **1776** and the Declaration of Independence. It ends with the hope that "government of the people, by the people, and for the people, shall not perish from the earth."
