**The Eagle, Globe, and Anchor (EGA)** is the Marine Corps emblem, adopted in **1868**. It drew from Britain's Royal Marines but was made American:

- **Eagle** — the nation Marines swear to protect.
- **Globe** (showing the Western Hemisphere) — Marines can be called to fight anywhere, anytime.
- **Anchor** — the Corps' naval heritage: Marines fight from the sea.

A Marine first receives the EGA at the Eagle, Globe, and Anchor Ceremony — the moment that signifies a person has become a Marine. It appears on everything from the Marine Corps flag to Marines' boots.

**The blood stripe** is the red stripe down the outer seam of the dress blue trousers of **officers and NCOs** — 1½ inches wide for NCOs and 2 inches for officers.

**Myth vs. fact:** a popular story says the stripe honors the Marines killed storming **Chapultepec** in the Mexican-American War (1847). The Marine Corps itself says that story is "more fable than fact": the stripe was part of the uniform **several years before** that battle. Today it is a tribute to the sacrifice and courage Marines have shown in every battle since 1775.

**Lesson for any researcher:** a good story isn't always a true one — check the source.
