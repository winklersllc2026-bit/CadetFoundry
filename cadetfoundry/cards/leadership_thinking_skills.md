**Critical thinking** (para 3-49) **questions what you see, hear, read or experience.** It means analyzing, comparing, weighing evidence and drawing conclusions — and having the self-discipline to **avoid jumping to conclusions.** Questions to ask:
- **Who or what is the source** of this idea?
- **What is the evidence for and against** it?
- What are **other explanations** or points of view?
- **Do the facts agree with other sources?**
- **How likely is this?**

These are your best defense against rumors, fake posts and scams.

**Creative thinking** (paras 3-50, 3-51) looks at problems **from a fresh perspective** — creating new ideas or combining old ones. Two tricks:
- **Find hidden assumptions** — what are we taking for granted?
- **Imagine failure** — "What could make this plan fail?" This protects a team against **groupthink** and hasty agreement.

**Reflective thinking** (para 3-52) builds understanding by thinking an experience through. Ask (table 3-2):
- **What does this information mean?**
- **What is the real problem?**
- **What don't I know that I should?**
- How does this fit with what I already know?

Thinking well takes **practice**.

## Quiz
Q: What does critical thinking do? | A: Questions what you see, hear, read or experience, weighing evidence before drawing conclusions | WRONG: Criticizes other people's ideas; Accepts what you hear without question; Makes quick guesses
Q: Name two critical thinking questions | A: Who or what is the source, what is the evidence for and against, what are other explanations, do the facts agree with other sources, how likely is this | WRONG: What do my friends think, what is most popular; What is fastest, what is easiest; Who said it, is it famous
Q: What is creative thinking? | A: Looking at problems from a fresh perspective to come up with new ideas | WRONG: Copying what worked before; Following the plan exactly; Drawing pictures
Q: What does imagining failure protect a team against? | A: Groupthink and hasty agreement | WRONG: Success; Hard work; Long meetings
Q: What is a hidden assumption? | A: Something a plan takes for granted without saying so | WRONG: A secret plan; A mistake you hide from leaders; A fact everyone checked
Q: What is reflective thinking? | A: Thinking an experience through to understand it and form conclusions | WRONG: Looking in a mirror; Thinking about the future only; Deciding fast without thinking
Q: Name a reflective thinking question | A: What does this information mean, what is the real problem, what don't I know that I should | WRONG: What time is it, what's for lunch; Who is to blame, how do I avoid trouble; How fast can I finish this
