A **bill** is a proposal for a new law or a change to an existing law. The idea can come from a member of Congress, a campaign promise, or citizens who petition for it.

**The steps:**
1. A member of Congress introduces the bill.
2. It goes to a **committee**, which studies it, debates it, and may change (amend) it.
3. That chamber (the House or the Senate) **votes** on it.
4. The **other chamber** goes through the same process.
5. The two chambers work out any differences, and both vote on the same text.
6. The bill goes to the **President**, who can **sign** it into law or **veto** (reject) it.
7. Congress can vote to **override** a veto.

**Pocket veto:** if the President doesn't sign a bill and Congress adjourns, the bill doesn't become law — and that veto can't be overridden.

Citizens take part too: you can contact your representative and senators about any bill.

## Quiz
Q: What is a bill? | A: A proposal for a new law or a change to an existing law
Q: Where does a bill go first after it is introduced? | A: To a committee | WRONG: To the President; To the Supreme Court; To a vote of the people
Q: How many chambers of Congress must pass a bill? | A: Both — the House and the Senate | ALSO: does a bill need to pass both the house and senate | WRONG: Only the House; Only the Senate; Neither — the President decides
Q: What can the President do with a bill Congress passes? | A: Sign it into law or veto it
Q: What is a veto? | A: When the President rejects a bill | WRONG: When the Supreme Court approves a law; When Congress passes a bill; When a state refuses a federal law
Q: Can Congress override a veto? | A: Yes — Congress can vote to override it
Q: What is a pocket veto? | A: When the President doesn't sign a bill and Congress adjourns, so it doesn't become law | WRONG: When the President signs a bill without telling anyone; When Congress overrides a veto; When a bill is sent back to committee
