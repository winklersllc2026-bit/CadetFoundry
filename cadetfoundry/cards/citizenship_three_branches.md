**Why three branches:** the Constitution of the United States divides the federal government into three branches. This ensures that no individual or group will have too much power.

**Legislative branch — Congress** (the Senate and the House of Representatives):
- drafts proposed laws
- confirms or rejects the president's nominations
- surveys the budget
- has the authority to declare war

**Executive branch — the president, the vice president and the president's cabinet:**
- the president is the head of state and leader of the executive branch
- the vice president supports the president and breaks ties in Senate votes
- cabinet members advise the president

**Judicial branch — the Supreme Court and other federal courts:**
- interprets what laws mean
- applies laws to individual cases
- decides if laws violate the Constitution

**Checks and balances:** each branch can respond to the actions of the others. The president can veto legislation; Congress confirms or rejects the president's nominees and can remove the president from office in exceptional circumstances; the Supreme Court can overturn unconstitutional laws.
