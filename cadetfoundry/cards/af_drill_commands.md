**Drill standards** (para 1.3.1): a **24-inch step** and a cadence of **100 to 120 steps per minute**. For drill, Air Force and Space Force organizations are divided into **elements, flights, squadrons, groups and wings**.

**Two-part commands** (para 2.1.1):
- The **preparatory command** says what the movement will be ("**Flight**," in Flight, HALT).
- The **command of execution** says when ("**HALT**").
- Some combine both: **FALL IN, AT EASE, REST**.
- **AS YOU WERE** cancels a preparatory command.

**The command voice** (para 2.3): loud and distinct enough for everyone to hear, with the snap that "demands a willing, correct, and immediate response." The voice comes from the diaphragm — don't strain your vocal cords.

**Attention** (para 3.2): heels together, feet at **45 degrees**, legs straight without locking the knees, thumbs along the trouser seams, hands cupped, eyes to the front, silent and still.

**Parade rest** (para 3.3.1.1): the left foot moves so the heels are **12 inches apart**; hands behind the back, right hand in the palm of the left, thumbs forming an "X."

**Facings** (para 3.4): right, left and about face are **two-count** movements done from attention at quick time.

**Learning "BY THE NUMBERS"** (para 1.6.3): a movement is practiced one count at a time until the instructor says "WITHOUT THE NUMBERS."

## Quiz
Q: How long is the Air Force drill step? | A: 24 inches
Q: What is the Air Force drill cadence? | A: 100 to 120 steps per minute
Q: In "Flight, HALT," which part is the command of execution? | A: HALT | WRONG: Flight; Both words; Neither — it's a combined command
Q: What command cancels a preparatory command? | A: As you were
Q: At Air Force parade rest, how far apart are the heels? | A: 12 inches
Q: What is it called when a movement is practiced one count at a time? | A: By the numbers | WRONG: In cadence; At ease; Route step
