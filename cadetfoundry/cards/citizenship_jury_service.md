The Bill of Rights promises a **trial by jury** — and that only works if citizens serve. Jury service is one of the ways ordinary people take part in the justice system.

**To serve on a federal jury, a person must:**
- be a **U.S. citizen**
- be at least **18 years old**
- have lived mainly in the judicial district for at least **one year**
- be able to read, write, understand and speak **English** well enough
- have no disqualifying mental or physical condition that can't be addressed with an accommodation
- not currently be charged with a felony punishable by more than one year in prison
- never have been convicted of a felony (unless their civil rights were legally restored)

State courts set their own rules for state juries, and they are often similar.

If you get a jury summons, **don't ignore it** — it's an official court order. Follow its instructions, and contact the court if you have a conflict.
