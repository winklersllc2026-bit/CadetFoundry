**Key dates:**
- **1959–1960**: the Air Force and Lockheed build the first spacecraft control center in California and write the first manuals for tracking and commanding satellites.
- **September 1982**: **Air Force Space Command** is activated.
- **September 1985**: **U.S. Space Command (USSPACECOM)** is created as a joint command.
- **1990–1991**: in Operations **Desert Shield and Desert Storm**, the full range of space capabilities is used in combat for the first time.
- **October 2002**: after 9/11, USSPACECOM is disbanded and its space duties move to **U.S. Strategic Command**.
- **Later**: China's antisatellite test and Russian "nesting-doll" spacecraft show that space is becoming **contested**.
- **February 19, 2019**: **Space Policy Directive 4** lays out the plan for a new service.
- **August 29, 2019**: **U.S. Space Command is re-established**.
- **December 20, 2019**: the NDAA turns Headquarters Air Force Space Command into Headquarters **U.S. Space Force** and creates the **Chief of Space Operations**.

**Space Force or Space Command?**
- The **U.S. Space Force** is a military **service**, like the Navy or the Army. It **organizes, trains and equips** Guardians.
- **U.S. Space Command** is a joint **combatant command**. It **fights** with forces from all the services, including the Space Force.
- Like every service, the Space Force **presents** its ready forces to combatant commanders.

**Today.** The Space Force headquarters is in the **Pentagon**. Its major installations are in **California, Colorado and Florida**. In 2024 it had more than **14,000** military and civilian Guardians. It sits under the Department of the Air Force much like the Marine Corps sits under the Department of the Navy.
