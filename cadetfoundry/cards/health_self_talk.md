**Mental readiness** has three connected parts (para 9-8): **cognitive, emotional and interpersonal.** Like fitness, it can be trained.

**Goal, Plan, Do, Check** (paras 9-9 to 9-13)
1. **Goal** — set a SMART goal for a specific task.
2. **Plan** — how you'll get there.
3. **Do** — practice and put the plan into action.
4. **Check** — did it work? If not, adjust and try again.

*FM 7-22 example:* "In 3 weeks, I will increase my sleep from 5 hours to 6 hours on at least 5 nights a week" — by being in bed by 2200 and **putting devices away 30 minutes before bed.**

**Self-talk** (paras 9-42 to 9-44) — what you say to yourself changes how you perform. Thoughts like *"I've never been a runner"* lead to a defeatist attitude and less effort. Three productive kinds (table 9-5):
- **Instructional** — when learning a skill: short cues like *"inhale, knees out, exhale and squat."*
- **Motivational** — before or during an event: *"I have studied, and I am ready."*
- **Cognitive reframing** — see a bad situation as a chance to learn: *"Hills are my friend. They only make me stronger."*

**Interpersonal readiness** (paras 9-45, 9-46) — healthy, trusting relationships. When you face stress, it's much easier to cope **with the support of your team, family or community.** Poor communication leads to isolation.

## Quiz
Q: What are the three parts of mental readiness? | A: Cognitive, emotional and interpersonal | WRONG: Physical, nutritional and sleep; Speed, strength and stamina; Reading, writing and math
Q: What are the four steps of Goal, Plan, Do, Check? | A: Set a goal, make a plan, do it, check whether it worked | WRONG: Pick a goal, skip the plan, try it once, give up if it fails; Set a goal, tell everyone, wait, celebrate; Make a list, lose it, guess, hope
Q: What does negative self-talk like "I've never been a runner" lead to? | A: A defeatist attitude and less effort | WRONG: Faster times and more confidence; Better sleep; More effort and better results
Q: Name the three kinds of productive self-talk | A: Instructional, motivational and cognitive reframing
Q: What is cognitive reframing? | A: Seeing a bad situation as a chance to learn or grow | WRONG: Pretending a problem doesn't exist; Blaming someone else for a bad situation; Changing the frame of your glasses
Q: When do you use instructional self-talk? | A: When learning or perfecting a skill | WRONG: When you're trying to fall asleep; When you're angry at a teammate; Only after you've failed
Q: Why does support from your team, family or community matter? | A: It makes it much easier to cope with stress | WRONG: It means you never have to try hard; It lets others do your work for you; It doesn't matter for stress
