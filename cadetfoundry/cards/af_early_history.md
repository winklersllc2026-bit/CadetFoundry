**August 1, 1907: the beginning.** The Army's Chief Signal Officer created the **Aeronautical Division** of the Signal Corps, under **Capt. Charles D. Chandler**. It handled "all matters pertaining to military ballooning, air machines and kindred subjects." It is the first organization the Air Force can trace an **unbroken lineage** to.

**1909: the first military airplane.** The Army bought the **Wright 1909 Military Flyer** for **$30,000** on **August 2, 1909**. It was named **Signal Corps Airplane No. 1** and was the Army's **only** airplane for almost two years.
- Its top speed was **42 mph**.
- **Wilbur Wright** taught the first Army pilots to fly it.
- In 1910, **Lt. Benjamin Foulois** taught himself to fly in it at Fort Sam Houston, Texas.

**1947**: the Air Force became a separate service.

**1948–1949: the Berlin Airlift.** In **June 1948**, the Soviets blocked all roads and rails into **West Berlin**. The new U.S. Air Force, with the British and French, flew in fuel, food and supplies. The museum calls it a "**464-day effort to supply a city's needs solely through the air**."
- The blockade ended on **May 12, 1949**.
- The airlift kept flying until **September 1949**.

The museum calls it "an early triumph for the young U.S. Air Force" and one of the defining events of the **Cold War**.
