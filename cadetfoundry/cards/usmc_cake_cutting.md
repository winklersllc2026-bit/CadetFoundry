Every year around **10 November**, Marines celebrate the Corps' birthday with a ball and a formal **cake cutting ceremony**. The manual calls it "an annual renewal of each Marine's commitment to the Corps."

**What happens:**
- The adjutant reads **General John A. Lejeune's birthday message**. Lejeune, the 13th Commandant, ordered on **1 November 1921** that it be published to all Marines every year.
- The cake is rolled in on a cart with a Marine sword. The **oldest and youngest Marine present** follow it.
- The commanding officer cuts the cake with the **Mameluke sword**, a reminder that Marines are "a band of warriors" who carry the sword so the nation may live in peace. The band plays **"Auld Lang Syne."**
- **First piece** goes to the **guest of honor**.
- **Second piece** goes to the **oldest Marine**, as a sign of honor and respect for experience. The oldest Marine takes a bite and passes it to the **youngest Marine**.

**Why oldest to youngest?** It stands for passing on **wisdom, knowledge and experience**, and for trust and confidence in the Marines who will carry the Corps' traditions into the future.

At bases without a general officer, and at enlisted or unit balls, the ceremony may be changed to fit local conditions.
