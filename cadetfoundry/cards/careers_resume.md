A **resume** is a short summary of your qualifications. A person typically scans it for only **15 to 20 seconds** — so make your qualifications easy to find.

**Top priority:** show you meet the job's **minimum qualifications**, near the top, using words that match the job posting.

**Format:** **1–2 pages**, clean and easy to read, with white space.

**Typical order:**
1. **Contact information**
2. (Optional) a **summary** or highlights — a good place for volunteer work, school accomplishments and community service
3. **Education and training** — near the top if you're a new graduate
4. **Work history** — most recent job first, working backward

**For a first resume:** volunteer service, leadership roles, awards and activities count. Your Cadet Foundry portfolio (service hours, leadership, recognition) can help you remember what you've done.

**Always have someone review it** for spelling, grammar and how well it fits the job. American Job Centers offer free resume feedback.

**Using AI:** it can suggest wording or compare your resume to a job posting — but **draft it in your own voice first and edit anything AI writes** so the final resume sounds like you and is true.

Q: How long should a resume be? | A: One to two pages | WRONG: Five to six pages; As long as possible; One paragraph
Q: What is the top priority of a resume? | A: Showing you meet the job's minimum qualifications | WRONG: Showing off your hobbies; Listing every job you've ever wanted; Using as many colors and fonts as possible
Q: In work history, which job goes first? | A: Your most recent job | WRONG: Your oldest job; Your favorite job; Your shortest job
Q: Where should education go if you're a new graduate? | A: Near the top | WRONG: At the very bottom; On a separate page; Nowhere — leave it off
Q: What should you always do before sending your resume? | A: Have someone review it for spelling, grammar and fit | WRONG: Add a selfie at the top; Make the font as small as possible to fit more; Send it to every company at once without reading it
