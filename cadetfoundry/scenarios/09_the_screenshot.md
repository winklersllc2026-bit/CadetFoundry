Title: The screenshot
Competency: Online conduct
Situation:
Someone in your company posts an unflattering photo of a Cadet who tripped during drill, with a mocking caption. It's getting likes and laughing emojis. The Cadet in the photo hasn't said anything, but they skipped lunch with the group today. You're a team leader, and two of your Cadets have already reposted it.
Choice: Ignore it — it's just a joke and it'll blow over.
Outcome: The post keeps spreading. The Cadet in the photo starts making excuses to miss after-school practice. Your two Cadets think reposting was fine because nobody said anything.
Strengths: You didn't add to it yourself.
Weaknesses: Silence from a leader looks like approval. The Cadet being mocked feels alone, and your team learns that this is acceptable.
Choice: Comment publicly calling out the person who posted it.
Outcome: The thread turns into an argument. More people see the photo because of the fight. The poster gets defensive and doubles down.
Strengths: You made it clear you don't support mocking a teammate.
Weaknesses: A public fight can make the situation bigger and put more eyes on the Cadet you meant to protect.
Choice: Privately ask your two Cadets to take their reposts down, ask the poster to delete it, and check in with the Cadet in the photo.
Outcome: Your Cadets take theirs down — one says they didn't think about how it felt. The poster deletes it after a day. The Cadet in the photo thanks you quietly.
Strengths: You led your own team first, protected the Cadet's dignity, and didn't spread the post further.
Weaknesses: It takes several conversations, and you can't control everyone who already saved it.
Principles:
- Think, Type, and Post: think about who could see it, type what matches your values, post only what shows dignity and respect.
- Online bullying and harassment are misconduct the same as in person.
- Leaders are accountable for what they do and what they fail to do.
Other approaches:
If the post doesn't come down or it keeps happening, tell your instructor. Mocking a teammate online is bullying, and adults can step in in ways you can't.
Reflection: Have you ever reposted something without thinking about the person in it? What would you want a leader to do if it were you?
Notice: Likes and reposts make bystanders part of what happened — including Cadets on your own team.
Discussion:
- Why might calling out the poster publicly make things worse?
- What responsibility does a team leader have for what their Cadets repost?
- How could you check on the Cadet in the photo without making it bigger?
Follow-up: Before posting anything this week, Cadets ask: who could see this, and does it show dignity and respect? Share one example next class (no names).
