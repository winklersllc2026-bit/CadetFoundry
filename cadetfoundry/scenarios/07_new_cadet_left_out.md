Title: The new Cadet
Competency: Inclusion
Situation:
A new Cadet transferred to your school mid-year and joined your platoon. At lunch and during breaks, they sit alone. During a team activity, nobody picks them, and you hear two Cadets joking about how they talk.
Choice: Let it be — they'll make friends on their own eventually.
Outcome: Weeks pass. The new Cadet stops coming to optional events. The jokes continue.
Strengths: You didn't force anything.
Weaknesses: Doing nothing tells everyone the jokes are acceptable. Leaders set the climate.
Choice: Invite the new Cadet to sit with your group and pair them with someone for the next activity.
Outcome: It's a little awkward at first. Over the next week they start talking more, and one of your friends finds they have a lot in common.
Strengths: A small action from a leader changes who feels welcome.
Weaknesses: It doesn't address the jokes on its own.
Choice: Tell the two Cadets privately that the jokes need to stop, and include the new Cadet in your group.
Outcome: The two Cadets roll their eyes but the jokes stop around you. The new Cadet starts showing up to events. Your squad notices what you did.
Strengths: You set the standard and made someone feel they belong.
Weaknesses: It takes nerve to correct peers, and they may not like it at first.
Principles:
- Respect: treat people as they should be treated (one of the Army Values).
- Leaders are responsible for the climate of their team, not just the tasks.
- Correct behavior privately; include people publicly.
Other approaches:
Do both: welcome the new Cadet and quietly address the jokes. If teasing turns into bullying, tell your instructor.
Reflection: What would you do differently next time? What did it feel like when you were the new person somewhere?
Notice: The jokes and the empty lunch seat are the same problem: the team's climate, which the leader shapes.
Discussion:
- Why is 'they'll make friends on their own' tempting, and what does it cost?
- Why correct the jokes privately but include the new Cadet publicly?
- What small things make someone feel part of a team in their first week?
Follow-up: As a class, list three things your platoon could do for any new Cadet in their first week, and pick one to start.
