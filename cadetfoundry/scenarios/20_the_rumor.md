Title: The rumor
Competency: Critical thinking
Situation:
A post is going around your school's group chats: a screenshot claiming the JROTC program is being cut next year and the instructors are being let go. People are upset, and some younger Cadets are talking about quitting now. A friend asks you, as a squad leader, to share it to "get the word out."
Choice: Share it — if it's true, people deserve to know.
Outcome: More Cadets get upset. Two quit. A week later it turns out the screenshot was from a different school district years ago. The Cadets who quit don't come back.
Strengths: You wanted to keep people informed.
Weaknesses: You spread something you hadn't checked, and real people made decisions based on it.
Choice: Tell your friend it's fake and ignore it.
Outcome: You happen to be right, but you didn't actually know. The rumor keeps spreading in other chats, and the younger Cadets stay worried.
Strengths: You didn't add to the rumor.
Weaknesses: You dismissed it without checking, and didn't help the people who were worried.
Choice: Don't share it. Ask yourself where it came from and what the evidence is, then ask your instructor directly. Share the real answer with your squad.
Outcome: Your instructor says the program isn't being cut and points out the screenshot is from another district. You tell your squad the facts and where you got them. The younger Cadets calm down.
Strengths: You checked the source, went to someone who actually knew, and stopped the rumor with facts.
Weaknesses: It took a little time while people were still upset.
Principles:
- Critical thinking: who or what is the source? What is the evidence? Do the facts agree with other sources?
- Think, Type, and Post: think about the message and who could see it before you share.
- Leaders keep their team informed with accurate information.
Other approaches:
When you're not sure something is true, say so: "I haven't confirmed that — I'm asking the instructor."
Reflection: Have you ever shared something that turned out to be false? How could you check next time before you share?
Notice: A squad leader's share carries more weight than a stranger's — so checking first matters more.
Discussion:
- How can you tell where a screenshot came from?
- Why is calling it fake without checking also a problem?
- How should a leader keep their squad informed while the facts aren't clear?
Follow-up: Bring one recent post (no names) and, as a class, ask the three questions: who's the source, what's the evidence, do other sources agree?
