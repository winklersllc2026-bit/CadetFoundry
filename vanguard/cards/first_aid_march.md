**Tactical Combat Casualty Care (TCCC)** goes after the most common causes of preventable death in combat: **bleeding, tension pneumothorax and airway problems**. Its three goals: **treat the casualty, prevent additional casualties, and complete the mission** (para 15-1).

**Three phases of care** (paras 15-3 to 15-5):
1. **Care under fire** (care under threat) — the threat is still active. **Massive bleeding is the only medical priority.** Return fire, get to cover, and stop massive bleeding (paras 15-6, 15-7).
2. **Tactical field care** — the threat is neutralized. Assess the casualty; reassess bleeding; treat airway, breathing and other injuries.
3. **Tactical evacuation care** — during and after the move to a higher level of care.

**MARCH PAWS** in tactical field care (para 15-9). During threat to life:
- **M — Massive bleeding:** control it with at least one tourniquet. A tourniquet goes on arterial bleeding of an arm or leg **high and tight, over the uniform**. Still bleeding? Apply a **second tourniquet above the first**. Where a tourniquet can't go, use **combat gauze**; control other bleeding with a pressure dressing (para 15-10).
- **A — Airway:** open it. No face or skull injury — **chin-lift**; with those injuries — **jaw thrust** (para 15-11).
- **R — Respiration:** treat open and sucking chest wounds with a **vented chest seal** (para 15-12).
- **C — Circulation:** reassess every tourniquet and treat for **shock** — signs include clammy (sweaty but cool) skin, pale skin, restlessness, unusual thirst, rapid breathing (para 15-13).
- **H — Hypothermia / Head injuries:** keep the casualty dry and warm; watch a head injury for declining mental status (para 15-14).

After threat to life: **P**ain, **A**ntibiotics, **W**ounds (dress them), **S**plints (splint fractures).

This card is for study only. First aid is a hands-on skill — learn it from your instructors and medics, practice it, and follow their current training.
