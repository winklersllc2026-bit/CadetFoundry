**FAFSA** stands for the **Free Application for Federal Student Aid**. You use it to apply for financial aid for **college, career school or graduate school**.

**What it's used for:** colleges and career schools use it to decide how much aid you can get — **grants, scholarships, work-study funds and loans**. Some **states and schools** also use it for their own grants, scholarships and loans.

**Who should fill it out:** **any student, regardless of income**, who wants to be considered for federal, state and school aid. Don't skip it because you think your family makes too much.

**Good to know:**
- People typically finish their section in **12 minutes or less**.
- You need an **FSA ID** (your verified account username and password), a parent contributor's email address, and income information if required.
- **Apply every year** you're in school.
- **Check your state's deadline** — some state aid has early deadlines. Missing a state deadline doesn't affect federal aid, so fill it out even if your state's deadline has passed.

**Grants vs. loans:** most **grants generally don't have to be repaid** — unlike loans. The biggest is the **Federal Pell Grant**, for undergraduates with exceptional financial need: up to **$7,395** for 2026–27. You might have to repay part of a grant if, for example, you withdraw from school early.

The FAFSA is **free** at studentaid.gov — never pay a website to file it for you. Your school counselor can help.
