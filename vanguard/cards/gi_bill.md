**Post-9/11 GI Bill (Chapter 33)** — helps pay for school or job training. You may be eligible if **at least one** is true:
- you served **at least 90 days on active duty** (all at once or with breaks) on or after September 11, 2001, or
- you received a Purple Heart on or after that date and were honorably discharged, or
- you served at least 30 continuous days and were honorably discharged with a service-connected disability.

**What it can pay for:** tuition and fees (the full cost of public, in-state tuition at the maximum benefit), a **monthly housing allowance** if you're in school more than half time, money for books and supplies, tutoring, national tests, licensing and certification tests, work-study, and vocational training, apprenticeships and on-the-job training.

**How much:** up to **36 months** of entitlement. Your percentage depends on how much active duty you've served since September 10, 2001.

**Does it expire?** If your service ended **on or after January 1, 2013**, your benefits **don't expire** (the Forever GI Bill). Service that ended before then: 15 years.

**Good to know:**
- No housing allowance during school breaks.
- You must **verify your enrollment every month** to keep getting housing payments.
- A qualified service member can **transfer** all or part of the 36 months to a spouse or child — the Defense Department approves transfers.
- The **Yellow Ribbon Program** can help with private or out-of-state tuition the GI Bill doesn't cover.

**Montgomery GI Bill Active Duty (Chapter 30):** you may qualify if, among other requirements, you entered active duty after June 30, 1985, had your pay reduced **$100 a month for the first 12 months**, and served continuously for **3 years** (or **2 years** if that was your enlistment agreement).

**The big difference:** the Post-9/11 GI Bill pays **tuition and fees to the school** and adds a **housing allowance** and books money. The Montgomery GI Bill pays a **set monthly amount to you**, and you pay the school yourself; it has **no separate housing allowance**. Each gives up to **36 months**; if you're eligible for both, you may get up to **48 months in total**, not 48 from one.

Apply on **va.gov**. Check your own eligibility with VA or your education center.
