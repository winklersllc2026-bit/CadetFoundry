**Individual movement techniques** (para 7-2): besides walking, there are three — the **low crawl, high crawl, and rush**.

- **Low crawl** (para 7-3) — the **lowest silhouette**. Use it where cover and concealment are very low and enemy fire or observation keeps you from getting up. Body flat; grasp the sling at the upper sling swivel, front hand guard resting on your forearm (muzzle off the ground), butt dragging. Push your arms forward, pull your firing-side leg forward, then pull with your arms and push with your leg.
- **High crawl** (para 7-4) — **faster than the low crawl** and still low. Use it when there's good cover and concealment but enemy fire keeps you from getting up. Body off the ground on forearms and lower legs, weapon cradled with the muzzle up, knees well behind your buttocks. Move right elbow with left knee, then left elbow with right knee.
- **Rush** (para 7-7) — the **fastest** way between positions. Each rush lasts **3 to 5 seconds**, so enemy gunners can't track you — but always try to hit the ground behind cover, not in the open just because 5 seconds passed. Pick your next covered and concealed position and route first. From the prone: slowly raise your head and pick your spot, slowly lower it, draw your arms in, pull your right leg forward, straighten your arms, get up quickly, and rush.
- If you've been firing from one spot for a while, roll or crawl a short distance before you rush — the enemy may be aiming where they last saw you (para 7-6).

**Cover vs. concealment** (chapter 5):
- **Cover** protects you — from bullets, fragments, flame, nuclear, biological and chemical effects, **and** observation.
- **Concealment** only **hides** you from observation. It does **not** protect you from enemy fire.

**Camouflage** keeps you, your equipment and your position from being identified. Change and improve it often (chapter 5). Things that give you away:
- **Movement** — draws attention; move only when necessary, on covered and concealed routes (5-8).
- **Positions** — don't put anything where the enemy expects it: avoid open areas, road junctions and lone buildings (5-9).
- **Outlines and shadows** — break them up; stay in the shadows when moving (5-10).
- **Shine** — cigarettes and flashlights at night; polished surfaces and exposed skin by day. Cover skin with clothing and face paint (5-11).
- **Shape** — helmets and human outlines are easy to recognize; break them up, but don't overdo it (5-12).
- **Colors** — blend with the background (5-13).
- **Dispersion** — a bunch of Soldiers is easier to spot than one. Spread out (5-14).
