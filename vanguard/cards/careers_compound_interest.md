**Compound interest is the interest you earn on interest.**

**Example:** $100 earning 5% a year:
- End of year 1: **$105**
- End of year 2: **$110.25** — you earned $5 on your $100, plus **$0.25 on last year's $5 of interest.**
- Without adding another dime: **more than $162 in 10 years**, and **almost $340 in 25 years.**

**The Rule of 72** — a quick way to estimate how long it takes money to **double**: divide **72 by the interest rate**. At 9%, money doubles about every **8 years** (72 ÷ 9 = 8).

**Small savings add up.** A $1 candy bar every day costs **$365 a year**. Saved and invested at 5%, that $365 grows to **$465.84 in five years** and **$1,577.50 in 30 years**.

**Starting young is your biggest advantage** — your money has more time to compound.

**Two habits from Investor.gov:**
- If you buy on impulse, **wait 24 hours** before buying anything. You may not want it tomorrow.
- **Empty your pockets** at the end of each day and set the spare change aside.

Real investments don't grow at a fixed rate every year and can lose value — these examples show how compounding works.
