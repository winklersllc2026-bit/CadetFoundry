**The four Rules of Firearms Safety** (paras 1-6 to 1-14) apply to every weapon, in training and in combat:
1. **Treat every weapon as if it is loaded.**
2. **Never point the weapon at anything you do not intend to destroy.** Know where your muzzle is and keep the path to the target clear of friendly forces and noncombatants.
3. **Keep your finger straight and off the trigger until you are ready to fire.** You are the most important safety feature on any weapon — don't rely on the mechanical safety alone.
4. **Ensure positive identification of the target and its surroundings.** Know what is in front of and beyond the target. You are responsible for every bullet you fire.

A negligent discharge takes breaking **two** of these rules, or a weapon safety status (para 1-5). Range SOPs can add rules, but these four always apply.

**Weapon safety status (WSS)** (para 1-18):
- **Green — "Fully Safe":** weapon clear, no ammunition, chamber empty, selector on SAFE.
- **Amber — "Substantially Safe":** bolt forward, chamber empty, ammunition in the weapon. Not used in the live-fire events in TC 3-22.9.
- **Red — "Marginally Safe":** selector on SAFE, magazine locked in, a round in the chamber, bolt forward.
- **Black — "Not Safe":** target identified, selector on FIRE, finger on the trigger, engaging.

**Immediate action** (para 8-35) — you squeeze the trigger and hear the hammer fall with a "click" instead of a "bang":
1. **Tap** the bottom of the magazine firmly.
2. **Rack** — pull the charging handle and release it.
3. **Reassess** by continuing the shot process.
If immediate action doesn't work after **two attempts**, or the trigger won't move or feels like "mush," look in the chamber and go to **remedial action** (para 8-36).

Always follow the range's own safety briefing and the commands of the tower and your lane safety.
