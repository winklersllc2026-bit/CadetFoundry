**Who you salute** (AR 600-25, para 2-1b, f): in uniform, salute when you meet and recognize someone entitled to it. Salutes are exchanged between **officers — commissioned and warrant —** and enlisted personnel of all the U.S. Armed Forces. The President is saluted as Commander in Chief. You do not salute NCOs — corporals and sergeants of any grade.

**How** (para 2-1c): the **junior person salutes first**. A greeting such as "Good morning, sir" or "Good morning, ma'am" with the salute is encouraged. **Do not salute indoors, except when reporting to a superior officer.**

**When salutes are not required** (para 2-1i) — when the senior, the junior, or both are:
1. in civilian clothes
2. doing routine work the salute would interfere with
3. carrying things with both hands
4. on a work detail, or in sports or social events where saluting would be a safety hazard
5. in public places such as theaters, churches and public transportation
6. in the ranks of a formation

**Vehicles** (para 2-1d): salute officers in official vehicles when recognized. If you are driving a moving vehicle, don't start a salute.

**The flag and the anthem** (para B-1, table B-1): indoors, stand at attention. Outdoors in uniform, salute; in civilian clothes, put your right hand over your heart. In uniform and not in formation, at the first note of reveille or the national anthem, **face the flag and salute** — if you can't see the flag, face the music. When uncased Colors pass within **6 paces**, salute outdoors or stand at attention indoors.

**Addressing leaders** (TC 3-21.5, paras 4-5, 4-6): enlisted Soldiers stand at **Attention** when addressing officers and at **Parade Rest** when addressing NCOs.
