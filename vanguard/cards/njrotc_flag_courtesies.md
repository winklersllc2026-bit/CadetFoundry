**Customs and courtesies** are the traditional ways nations and people pay respect — to distinguished persons, to other nations, and to places of historical significance (Ch. 2, A).

**When the national anthem or "To the Colors" plays** (Ch. 2, C):
- **Outdoors in uniform:** stand at attention, face the flag (or the music if you can't see the flag), and salute from the **first note to the last**.
- **Outdoors in civilian clothes:** stand at attention facing the flag with your right hand over your heart (men remove their hats and hold them over the heart).
- **Indoors:** face the flag at attention with your hand over your heart. In uniform indoors, don't salute unless you're covered or under arms.
- No need to stand or salute for a ceremony on radio or TV.

**Other flag courtesies:**
- An **escorted flag** passing you outdoors: salute **six paces before** it reaches you and hold until it's **six paces past**.
- Don't salute flags on stationary flagstaffs except during morning and evening colors. Small handheld flags at parades aren't saluted.
- **Half-staff** means halfway between the top and bottom of the staff, to honor people of national importance who have died.

**Colors:** **morning colors** signals the **start** of the official duty day; **retreat** signals the **end** (Ch. 2, E).

**The Pledge of Allegiance** is not recited in military formations and ceremonies. At civilian events, uniformed Cadets outdoors stand at attention, face the flag, stay silent and salute; indoors they stand at attention facing the flag without saluting (Ch. 2, D).

## Quiz
Q: In uniform outdoors during the national anthem, how long do you hold your salute? | A: From the first note to the last
Q: When an escorted flag passes you, when do you start and stop saluting? | A: Six paces before it reaches you until six paces after it passes
Q: What does morning colors signal? | A: The start of the official duty day
Q: What does retreat signal? | A: The end of the official duty day
Q: Is the Pledge of Allegiance recited in military formations? | A: No
Q: Do you salute indoors in uniform during the national anthem? | A: No, unless you are covered or under arms
