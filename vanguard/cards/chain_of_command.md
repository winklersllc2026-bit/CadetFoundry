**The chain of command** passes orders from the highest level to the lowest quickly and with the least chance of misunderstanding. It also works upward: people use it to bring issues and problems to their leaders.

**Commanders are responsible for everything their unit does or fails to do.** They share out responsibility and authority to subordinate leaders — but they still keep overall responsibility.

**The Army chain of command, top to bottom (Table 4-1):**
President of the United States (Commander in Chief) → Secretary of Defense → Secretary of the Army → Chief of Staff of the Army → Army Command commander → corps → division → brigade → battalion → company/battery/troop commander → **platoon leader** → **squad leader** → **team leader**.

**The NCO support channel** runs alongside it: Sergeant Major of the Army → command sergeants major → **first sergeant** (company) → **platoon sergeant**. NCOs run day-to-day operations, train Soldiers, enforce standards and care for Soldiers and their families.

**Why it matters to you:** go to the leader closest to you first. Skipping the chain makes problems harder to solve and leaves your leaders out of the loop.

## Quiz
Q: Who is at the top of the Army chain of command? | A: The President of the United States, the Commander in Chief | ALSO: who is the commander in chief
Q: What is the chain of command for? | A: Passing orders down quickly and clearly, and bringing issues and problems up to leaders
Q: Is a commander still responsible after delegating a task? | A: Yes — commanders keep overall responsibility for everything their unit does or fails to do
Q: What channel runs alongside the chain of command? | A: The NCO support channel
Q: Who is the senior NCO of a company? | A: The first sergeant
Q: In a platoon, the platoon leader is paired with which NCO? | A: The platoon sergeant
