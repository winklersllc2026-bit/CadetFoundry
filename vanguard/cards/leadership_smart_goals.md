**Goals should be SMART** (para 1-67):

- **S**pecific — What specifically do I want to achieve?
- **M**easurable — How will I know I've reached it?
- **A**chievable — What resources do I need?
- **R**ealistic — Is this goal reasonable for me?
- **T**ime-bound — When will I finish each step?

**Break big goals into smaller steps.** A big goal — like getting a job in a field you have no experience in — is easier to reach as short-term steps (para 1-67).

**Plan for trouble** (para 1-68): *What parts of my plan might be difficult? What would I do about that?*

**Find your help** (para 1-69): *Who might be able to help me, and how?*

**Define success so others could see it** (para 3-21): success should be **observable and measurable**. Ask: *How will I know I was successful? What will other people notice?*

**Example**
- Not SMART: "Get better at running."
- SMART: "Cut my mile time from 9:30 to 8:45 by the spring fitness test by running three times a week, and log every run in Vanguard."

## Quiz
Q: What does SMART stand for? | A: Specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, time-bound
Q: What question checks if a goal is measurable? | A: How will I know I've reached it?
Q: What question checks if a goal is time-bound? | A: When will I finish each step?
Q: What should you do with a big goal? | A: Break it into smaller, short-term steps
Q: Is "get better at running" a SMART goal? | A: No — it isn't specific, measurable or time-bound
Q: Name a question that helps you plan for trouble | A: What parts of my plan might be difficult, and what would I do about that?
