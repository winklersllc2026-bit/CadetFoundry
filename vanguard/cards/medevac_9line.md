The same 9-line format is used to request **air and ground** medical evacuation. Send it by secure means when you can — but don't put a patient at more risk if you can't.

- **Line 1 — Location of the pickup site**: grid coordinates, **with the grid zone letters**.
- **Line 2 — Radio frequency, call sign and suffix** of the radio **at the pickup site** (not a relay).
- **Line 3 — Number of patients by precedence**: **A** Urgent · **B** Urgent-Surgical · **C** Priority · **D** Routine · **E** Convenience.
- **Line 4 — Special equipment required**: **A** None · **B** Hoist · **C** Extraction equipment · **D** Ventilator.
- **Line 5 — Number of patients by type**: **L** + number (litter) · **A** + number (ambulatory, sitting).
- **Line 6 — Security of the pickup site** (wartime): **N** No enemy troops · **P** Possibly enemy (approach with caution) · **E** Enemy in area (approach with caution) · **X** Enemy in area (armed escort required).
- **Line 7 — Method of marking the pickup site**: **A** Panels · **B** Pyrotechnic signal · **C** Smoke signal · **D** None · **E** Other.
- **Line 8 — Patient nationality and status**: **A** U.S. military · **B** U.S. citizen · **C** Non-U.S. military · **D** Non-U.S. citizen · **E** Enemy prisoner of war.
- **Line 9 — CBRN contamination** (wartime): **C** Chemical · **B** Biological · **R** Radiological · **N** Nuclear — include only when contamination exists.

**In peacetime**, line 6 is the number and type of wounds, injuries or illness, and line 9 is a terrain description of the pickup site.

**Precedence** (patient's medical condition is the only factor):
- **Urgent** — within a maximum of **1 hour**, to save life, limb or eyesight.
- **Urgent-Surgical** — within **1 hour**, needs far-forward surgery.
- **Priority** — within **4 hours**.
- **Routine** — within **24 hours**.
- **Convenience** — a matter of medical convenience, not necessity.

**Sending it**: call the unit that controls the evacuation assets and say, in the clear, **"I have a MEDEVAC request."** Say each line number in the clear. **Always send lines 1 through 5 on first contact** — lines 6 to 9 can follow while the aircraft or vehicle is on the way. If more than one category goes on line 3 or 5, say **"BREAK"** between them. End with **OVER**, then keep the radio on and listen.

Don't send the **color** of the smoke or panel in the request — the crew identifies the color when they arrive and your unit confirms it.

A **MIST** report (mechanism of injury, injury type, signs, treatment) is sometimes added to the request.

This card is for study only. Learn and practice the request with your unit and follow its SOP.
