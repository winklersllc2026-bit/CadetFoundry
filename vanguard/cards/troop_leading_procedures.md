Army small-unit leaders use the **troop leading procedures (TLP)** to plan and prepare for a mission. The same eight steps work for planning a JROTC event, a service project or a competition.

**The eight steps** (table 2-1):
1. **Receive the mission.**
2. **Issue a warning order** — give your team early notice so they can start preparing.
3. **Make a tentative plan.**
4. **Initiate movement** — start the things that take time.
5. **Conduct reconnaissance** — go see the place or get the facts yourself.
6. **Complete the plan.**
7. **Issue the operation order** — brief the full plan.
8. **Supervise and refine.**

Steps 3 through 8 can happen **in any order or at the same time** (para 2-2).

**The 1/3–2/3 rule** (para 2-3): the leader uses **one-third** of the available planning time and leaves **two-thirds** for the team to prepare. Don't use up all the time planning while your team waits.

**The warning order** (para 2-4) gives enough information to start preparing as soon as possible — what the task is, where, and the **timeline with critical times**.

**JROTC example — a color guard for a Friday game:** receive the request Monday; warning order Monday (who, when, uniform); tentative plan; check the field and the flagpole; complete the plan; brief it Wednesday; rehearse and inspect Thursday and Friday.
