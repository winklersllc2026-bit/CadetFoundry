The Department of Education reviewed the research on how people learn and remember. Its recommendations, with how strong the evidence is:

**1. Quiz yourself** — *strong evidence.* Being tested on material helps you learn and remember it — better than just re-reading. That's why Vanguard's quizzes make you **type the answer**: trying to recall it is what builds memory. (In Vanguard, a wrong answer while practicing is never a bad mark — it's how you find what to learn next.)

**2. Ask deep "why" and "how" questions** — *strong evidence.* Don't just memorize *what*. Ask *why does this work? how does it connect? what would happen if…?* and explain the answer in your own words.

**3. Space your studying out over time** — *moderate evidence.* Studying a little on several days beats cramming the night before. Come back to old material after a few days or weeks.

**4. Mix examples with practice** — *moderate evidence.* Look at a worked example, then try a problem yourself, then another example.

**5. Combine pictures with words** — *moderate evidence.* Diagrams, maps and charts alongside explanations help ideas stick.

**6. Connect abstract ideas to real ones** — *moderate evidence.* Link a principle to a real situation — like connecting "Respect" to how you treat a new Cadet.

**7. Use quizzes to find what you don't know yet**, and spend your study time there. Judge what you know **after a delay**, not right after studying — the guide calls this a delayed judgment of learning.

## Quiz
Q: According to the research, which works better — re-reading or quizzing yourself? | A: Quizzing yourself
Q: Which two study methods have strong evidence? | A: Quizzing yourself and asking deep explanatory questions
Q: Is it better to cram or to space your studying out? | A: Space it out over time
Q: What is a deep explanatory question? | A: A why or how question that makes you explain ideas in your own words
Q: When should you judge how well you know something? | A: After a delay, not right after studying
Q: How can quizzes help you plan your study time? | A: They show what you don't know yet, so you can focus there
