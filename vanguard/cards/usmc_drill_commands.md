**Why Marines drill.** The manual says close order drill teaches Marines "to obey orders and to do so immediately in the correct way." It is a foundation of discipline and **esprit de corps**. Drill also builds confidence in junior leaders, because they practice giving commands and controlling troops.

**"First on foot, and right on the line."** On 9 August 1876 the Secretary of the Navy gave the Marine Corps the **place of honor** in any Naval formation.

**Four types of commands:**
1. **Preparatory command**: says what movement is coming, for example "Forward," "Left" or "Platoon."
2. **Command of execution**: makes it happen, for example "MARCH," "FACE" or "ATTENTION."
3. **Combined command**: one command that does both jobs, for example "FALL IN," "AT EASE" or "REST."
4. **Supplementary command**: given by subordinate leaders so their own units act, for example squad leaders during "Column of Files From the Right."

**The command voice.**
- Give commands from the **position of attention** and face your troops.
- Commands are given at the rhythm of **112 to 120 beats per minute**.
- Preparatory commands **rise** in pitch. The command of execution is sharp and higher, "like the crack of a whip."
- Never raise a hand to your mouth to help project.

**Teaching drill: P.C.W.C.** stands for **Purpose** of the movement, number of **Counts**, **When** it may be executed, and the **Commands**. For unit movements it is **P.F.W.C.**, with **Formation** in place of Counts.

**"AS YOU WERE"** cancels a movement that has started but isn't finished.
