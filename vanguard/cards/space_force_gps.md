The **Global Positioning System (GPS)** is a U.S.-owned utility. It provides **positioning, navigation and timing (PNT)**. The **U.S. Space Force** develops, maintains and operates it.

**Three segments:**
1. **Space segment**: a constellation of at least **24** satellites sending one-way signals with their position and the exact time.
2. **Control segment**: ground stations around the world that track the satellites, keep them in the right orbits and adjust their clocks.
3. **User segment**: GPS receivers, such as your phone or a car's navigation system, which use the signals to work out position and time.

**The constellation:**
- GPS satellites fly in **medium Earth orbit**, about **20,200 km (12,550 miles)** up.
- Each satellite circles the Earth **twice a day**.
- The satellites are arranged in **six orbital planes**, so you can see **at least four satellites** from almost anywhere on Earth.
- The U.S. commits to at least **24 working satellites, 95% of the time**. The Space Force usually flies extras, and gps.gov reported **31** operational satellites in July 2023.

**Free for the world.** Civilian GPS service is **free to everyone, worldwide, all the time**. A separate military service is available to U.S. and allied forces.

**Why it matters.** According to the Space Force, GPS timing helps run financial networks, international commerce, cell phone networks and critical infrastructure. That is why the Space Force protects it.
