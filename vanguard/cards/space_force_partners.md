The Space Force is small, so it relies on partners.

**The Air Force.** Both services are in the **Department of the Air Force**. Congress told the Space Force to avoid duplicating functions the Air Force already provides. Airmen give Guardians:
- personnel services;
- base and facility support;
- medical, legal, chaplain, finance, contracting, public affairs and weather support;
- combat support when deployed.

SFDD-1 calls these Airmen "essential" to the Space Force mission.

**Other government agencies:**
- **NASA**: works with the Space Force on the civilian space program and space research.
- **NOAA** (Department of Commerce): provides environmental and weather data.
- **Office of Space Commerce**: handles **space traffic management**, working with the Space Force.
- **FAA**: safety of flight, plus public safety during launches and reentries.
- **NTIA and FCC**: manage the radio spectrum and communications.

**Allies and international partners.** SFDD-1 says mission success is "unattainable without robust foreign partnerships." Working with allies keeps space "safe, secure, stable, and sustainable."

**Commercial and academic partners.** Companies and universities bring new technology. The Space Force uses **hybrid space architectures**, mixing military and commercial systems, so that capabilities are more resilient.

Q: Which office handles space traffic management with the Space Force? | A: The Office of Space Commerce
Q: Which agency works with the Space Force on the civilian space program? | A: NASA
Q: What is a hybrid space architecture? | A: Mixing military and commercial space systems for resilience
