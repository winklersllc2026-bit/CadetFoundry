**Why we hold ceremonies.** A ceremony does one or more of these things:
1. Gives distinctive **honors** to national symbols or people on special occasions.
2. Shows how **proficient and well-trained** a unit is.
3. Builds **teamwork and pride** in Air Force and Space Force units.
4. Lifts public morale by showing the **strength and unity** of the military.

**A parade is a review with honors.** Retreat, when respect is paid to the U.S. flag, is a parade ceremony of its own.

**Order of precedence for U.S. troops in a ceremony:**
1. Cadets, U.S. Military Academy
2. Midshipmen, U.S. Naval Academy
3. Cadets, U.S. Air Force Academy
4. Cadets, U.S. Coast Guard Academy
5. Midshipmen, U.S. Merchant Marine Academy
6. Then the services: **Army, Marine Corps, Navy, Air Force, Space Force, Coast Guard**
7. Then the Guard and Reserve components

**Foreign troops** invited to a U.S. ceremony get the place of honor ahead of U.S. troops. Only a small U.S. guard of honor marches in front of them.

**The commander of troops' checklist** includes:
- setting the time, date and place;
- choosing the formation and the type of ceremony;
- choosing the people for command positions and the color guard;
- marking the parade field;
- arranging the band and any flyover;
- holding practice;
- announcing the uniform.

Q: What is a parade? | A: A review with honors
Q: Name two purposes of a military ceremony. | A: To honor national symbols or people, and to show proficiency and build teamwork and pride
Q: Which service academy's cadets march first in U.S. ceremonies? | A: The U.S. Military Academy
Q: In ceremonies, which service comes right after the Air Force? | A: The Space Force
Q: Where do invited foreign troops march in a U.S. ceremony? | A: In the place of honor, ahead of U.S. troops
