The Uniform Code of Military Justice (UCMJ) is the military's criminal law. It applies to you 24/7, on and off duty.

- **Article 15** — Nonjudicial punishment ("an Article 15"): a commander can punish minor offenses without a court-martial. You generally have the right to refuse it and demand trial by court-martial instead (unless attached to or embarked on a vessel). Talk to Trial Defense Service (TDS) — free military lawyers — before deciding.
- **Article 31** — Your rights: you cannot be forced to incriminate yourself, and you must be told what you're suspected of before questioning.
- **Article 86** — Absence without leave (AWOL), including failing to go to your appointed place of duty on time.
- **Article 89** — Disrespect toward a superior commissioned officer.
- **Article 91** — Insubordinate conduct toward a warrant officer, NCO or petty officer.
- **Article 92** — Failure to obey an order or regulation (including dereliction of duty).
- **Article 107** — False official statements.
- **Article 108** — Military property: loss, damage, destruction or wrongful disposition.
- **Article 112a** — Wrongful use or possession of controlled substances.
- **Article 120** — Rape and sexual assault.
- **Article 121** — Larceny (stealing).
- **Article 128** — Assault.
- **Article 134** — The General Article: conduct prejudicial to good order and discipline or that brings discredit on the armed forces.

This card is a study aid, not legal advice. If you are in trouble, ask for Trial Defense Service.
