Title: The vape pen
Competency: Peer pressure
Situation:
At a friend's house after a competition, a few Cadets from your team pass around a vape pen. One holds it out to you: "Come on, everyone does it. It's not like it's a cigarette." You're the team captain, and the youngest Cadet on the team is watching to see what you do.
Choice: Take one hit so you don't seem uptight, then put it down.
Outcome: Nobody makes a big deal of it. But the youngest Cadet takes a hit right after you, and next weekend the pen comes out again — with you expected to join.
Strengths: You avoided an awkward moment.
Weaknesses: Nicotine is highly addictive, and you just showed the youngest Cadet that the captain thinks it's fine. Leaders set the standard by what they do.
Choice: Lecture the group about how vaping is stupid and leave.
Outcome: You stay clean, but the group feels judged and gets defensive. The youngest Cadet stays behind because they don't want to look like a kid.
Strengths: You held your ground.
Weaknesses: Shaming people pushes them away, and you left the person who was watching you behind.
Choice: Say "No thanks, I'm good," change the subject, and invite the youngest Cadet to help you grab food in the kitchen.
Outcome: A friend rolls their eyes, but nobody pushes. The youngest Cadet follows you out. Later, one of your teammates quietly asks you how you'd even start quitting.
Strengths: You said no without drama, set the example, and gave someone else an easy way out.
Weaknesses: It still feels awkward, and you can't control what the others do.
Principles:
- Most e-cigarettes contain nicotine, which is highly addictive and can harm attention, learning, mood and impulse control in a teen's brain.
- Leaders set the example — someone is always watching what you do.
- Personal courage includes moral courage: doing the right thing when the group goes the other way.
Other approaches:
Have a simple "no" ready before situations like this. If a teammate wants to quit, point them to free help like SmokefreeTXT for Teens or a trusted adult.
Reflection: What makes saying no hard in a moment like this? What is your go-to way to say no?
Notice: The youngest Cadet is watching — the captain's choice teaches them what this team does.
Discussion:
- Why can a lecture backfire even when the leader is right?
- What makes a short, calm 'No thanks' powerful?
- How do you look out for a younger teammate without embarrassing them?
Follow-up: Cadets write two ways to say no and change the subject that would sound natural from them.
