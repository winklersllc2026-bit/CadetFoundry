Title: Doing it all yourself
Competency: Delegation
Situation:
You've just been made platoon sergeant. There's an inspection Monday. Your squad leaders keep asking what they can do, but you're worried they won't do things the way you would, so you've been checking every uniform and every locker yourself. It's Saturday, and you're far behind.
Choice: Keep doing it yourself — it's the only way it'll be right.
Outcome: You check most uniforms but run out of time. Your squad leaders feel you don't trust them. The inspection goes okay, and you're exhausted.
Strengths: You hold a high standard and take your responsibility seriously.
Weaknesses: You became the bottleneck. Your squad leaders didn't get to lead or learn.
Choice: Give each squad leader their squad to check, with a clear standard and a time to report back to you.
Outcome: The squad leaders find most problems and fix them. You spot-check two squads and catch one issue to coach. Everything's done by Sunday afternoon.
Strengths: You trusted your people, set a clear standard, and still checked the result.
Weaknesses: Their work might not match yours exactly the first time. That's the price of growing new leaders.
Choice: Tell the squad leaders to handle it however they want.
Outcome: Each squad uses a different standard. At inspection, one squad is sharp and another missed the basics.
Strengths: You gave your squad leaders room to lead.
Weaknesses: Delegating without a standard or a check isn't delegating — it's hoping.
Principles:
- Delegate the task, keep the responsibility.
- Give a clear standard, a deadline, and a check-in.
- Developing other leaders is part of your job.
Other approaches:
Show the standard once (one inspected uniform as an example), hand each squad leader their squad, and set a time to report. Spot-check, then praise what went well and coach what didn't.
Reflection: What would you do differently next time? What do you find hardest to hand off?
