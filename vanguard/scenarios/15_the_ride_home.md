Title: The ride home
Competency: Personal courage
Situation:
It's 11:30 on a Saturday night after a party. Your friend drove you there and is supposed to drive you and two other Cadets home. You saw your friend drink a couple of beers, and now they say, "I'm fine, it was hours ago. Let's go." Your curfew is midnight, and your parents don't know there was drinking at the party.
Choice: Get in — they seem okay, and you don't want to get in trouble for being late or for being at the party.
Outcome: On the way home your friend drifts over the center line and overcorrects. Nobody is hurt, but the car ends up in a ditch, and the police come. Everyone's parents find out anyway — in the worst way.
Strengths: You were trying to protect your friend and yourself from getting in trouble.
Weaknesses: Any amount of alcohol raises a teen driver's crash risk, and nighttime and weekend driving are already the most dangerous. Being late is a problem you can fix; a crash may not be.
Choice: Refuse to ride, but let the others go without you.
Outcome: You call home and get picked up. The other two Cadets ride with your friend and make it home, but you lie awake worried until they text.
Strengths: You kept yourself safe and made an honest call home.
Weaknesses: You knew the danger and didn't try to stop your friend or the others from getting in the car.
Choice: Tell your friend you're not riding with them, ask for their keys, and call a parent to drive all of you home.
Outcome: Your friend is angry and embarrassed. Your parent shows up, drives everyone home, and saves the hard conversation for the next day. A week later your friend admits they shouldn't have driven.
Strengths: You protected everyone — including your friend — and chose an honest call over a dangerous secret.
Weaknesses: It costs you socially for a while, and you'll have to answer for being at the party.
Principles:
- Never drive impaired, and never ride with a driver who is. Teen passengers also raise a teen driver's crash risk.
- Personal courage includes moral courage — doing the right thing when it's uncomfortable.
- Leaders look out for the people around them, not just themselves.
Other approaches:
Make a deal with a parent or trusted adult ahead of time: you'll call for a ride, no questions that night, and talk about it the next day.
Reflection: Who could you call at midnight for a safe ride? Have you talked with them about it?
