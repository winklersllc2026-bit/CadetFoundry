Title: The first job
Competency: Integrity at work
Situation:
You just started your first part-time job at a grocery store. A coworker who has been there a year shows you how to clock in for friends who are running late, "so nobody gets in trouble." Today they text you: "I'm 20 min late, clock me in?" Your manager trusts you, and you'll need them as a reference for your next job.
Choice: Clock them in — it's just 20 minutes, and you don't want to start off on a bad note with a coworker.
Outcome: It works this time. Two weeks later the manager compares the time clock to the security camera. Both of you are written up, and the manager says they're disappointed in you most, because you were new and they had trusted you.
Strengths: You were trying to be a good teammate.
Weaknesses: Clocking someone in when they aren't there is falsifying a record — it's dishonest and can get you fired. It also cost you your manager's trust and a good reference.
Choice: Ignore the text and say nothing.
Outcome: Your coworker is irritated and keeps asking on other days. You feel stuck every time your phone buzzes.
Strengths: You didn't do anything dishonest.
Weaknesses: The problem keeps coming back because you never said where you stand.
Choice: Text back: "Can't do that — but I'll tell the manager you're on your way if they ask." Then keep your word.
Outcome: Your coworker grumbles but shows up and clocks in themself. They stop asking. Months later your manager agrees to be a reference and calls you "dependable."
Strengths: You stayed honest, set a clear boundary, and still helped your coworker in an honest way.
Weaknesses: It may cool things off with that coworker for a while.
Principles:
- Integrity: do what's right, legally and morally — even when it's a small thing.
- Employment references carry the most weight with employers. Trust takes months to build and one bad choice to lose.
- Accountability means answering for what you do, not just what you're told to do.
Other approaches:
If coworkers keep pressuring you to break rules, talk to your manager about the rule itself — without making it personal.
Reflection: What would you want your first boss to say about you to your next employer?
