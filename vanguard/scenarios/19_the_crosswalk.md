Title: The crosswalk
Competency: Civic action
Situation:
There's no crosswalk on the busy road between your school and the bus stop most students use. Last week a car nearly hit a freshman. Your friends are angry and want to post a video calling out the city online. Your instructor mentions that the city council meets every other Tuesday and lets residents speak.
Choice: Post the angry video tagging the city and the mayor.
Outcome: The video gets a lot of views and comments, but most are arguments. Nobody from the city responds, and nothing changes at the road.
Strengths: You raised awareness and showed you care.
Weaknesses: Anger online rarely reaches the people who make the decision, and it can make them less willing to listen.
Choice: Decide it's an adult problem and leave it alone.
Outcome: Nothing changes. Students keep running across the road.
Strengths: You avoided a hassle.
Weaknesses: Citizens — including young ones — have a voice in local government. Staying silent means the decision-makers may never hear about the problem.
Choice: Gather facts — how many students cross, when, and what happened last week — write a short, respectful statement, and ask your instructor to help your group speak at the city council meeting.
Outcome: You're nervous, but you speak for two minutes and hand out a one-page summary. A council member asks the city traffic office to study the crossing. Months later a crosswalk and a sign go in.
Strengths: You used facts, went to the people who could fix it, and showed respect while still pressing for change.
Weaknesses: It took time, planning and courage, and change wasn't immediate.
Principles:
- Your state and local governments make many decisions closest to your daily life — and they hold public meetings so residents can be heard.
- Critical thinking: gather the evidence before you make your case.
- Leaders take initiative to solve problems instead of waiting for someone else.
Other approaches:
You can also write or call your city council member, or ask your school's administration to request the crossing with you.
Reflection: What's a problem in your community you'd like to see fixed? Who has the power to fix it, and how could you reach them?
