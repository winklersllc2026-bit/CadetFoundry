Title: The flag on the ground
Competency: Respect and initiative
Situation:
At a home football game, the wind knocks over a small flag display near the bleachers. A U.S. flag is lying on the wet ground. Some students walk past it, and one steps on the corner. You're there in civilian clothes with two other Cadets, who are laughing about something on a phone.
Choice: Figure it's not your job — the school staff will notice eventually.
Outcome: The flag lies there for the whole first half, muddy and stepped on. A veteran in the stands eventually picks it up and looks around, disappointed.
Strengths: None really — you avoided getting involved.
Weaknesses: You knew the standard and didn't act. Leadership doesn't wait for someone to assign it.
Choice: Tell your two friends to go pick it up.
Outcome: They grumble and do it, but they bunch it up and toss it on a bench.
Strengths: The flag is off the ground.
Weaknesses: You gave an order instead of setting the example, and the job wasn't done right.
Choice: Pick it up yourself right away, ask your friends to help shake it off and fold it, and let a staff member know the display fell.
Outcome: Your friends jump in. A staff member thanks you and sets the display back up. The veteran in the stands gives you a nod.
Strengths: You showed initiative, set the example, and handled the flag with respect.
Weaknesses: Hardly any — it took two minutes of your game.
Principles:
- The U.S. Flag Code says the flag should never touch anything beneath it, such as the ground.
- Leaders lead by example and show initiative without being told.
- A flag too worn or damaged to display should be retired in a dignified way, preferably by burning.
Other approaches:
If the flag is torn or badly soiled, give it to a staff member or your instructor so it can be cleaned or retired properly.
Reflection: Why do small acts of respect matter when nobody asked you to do them?
