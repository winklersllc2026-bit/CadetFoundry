Title: Behind schedule
Competency: Planning and time management
Situation:
You're in charge of your platoon's booth at the school fair. It opens in two days. Half the tasks aren't started: the sign isn't painted, the sign-up sheet isn't printed, and nobody has confirmed who's working which shift. Everyone assumed someone else had it.
Choice: Stay late and do as much as you can yourself.
Outcome: You get the sign painted, but shifts are still unassigned and two Cadets show up at the same time while one hour has nobody. You're worn out on the day.
Strengths: You led by example and showed you care about the result.
Weaknesses: The problem was organization, not effort. Working harder alone didn't fix who-does-what.
Choice: Call a ten-minute huddle: list every task, give each one a name and a deadline, and check back tomorrow.
Outcome: Everyone knows their job. Two tasks slip, but because you checked back, you catch them a day early and fix them. The booth opens on time.
Strengths: Clear tasks, clear owners, clear deadlines — and you followed up.
Weaknesses: Some Cadets may grumble about being assigned work. You have to follow up, not just assign.
Choice: Tell the instructor the booth won't be ready and ask to cancel.
Outcome: The instructor asks what your plan is. You don't have one yet. The booth goes ahead, but the instructor ends up organizing it.
Strengths: You were honest about the problem early.
Weaknesses: Bringing a problem without a plan hands your job to someone else.
Principles:
- When everyone owns a task, nobody does. Give every task one name.
- A plan isn't done until you've checked on it.
- Bring problems with a proposed solution.
- Duty: fulfill your obligations (one of the Army Values).
Other approaches:
Make a simple list on paper or a whiteboard: task, who, when. Ask Cadets to choose tasks first, then assign what's left. Set one short check-in each day until the fair.
Reflection: What would you do differently next time, and how would you catch the problem earlier?
