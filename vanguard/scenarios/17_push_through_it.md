Title: Push through it
Competency: Looking out for your team
Situation:
The raider challenge team is two weeks out from competition. You lead the team. During a practice run, your best runner starts limping and tells you quietly, "My shin has hurt bad for a week. Don't tell the instructor — I'll push through it. If I sit out, we lose." Two other team members overheard and are watching to see what you'll do.
Choice: Keep their secret and let them keep training hard — they know their own body.
Outcome: The runner keeps going until the pain forces them to stop completely a week later. A doctor says it's a stress injury and they need six weeks off. They miss the competition anyway, and the other two team members learn that hiding pain is what this team expects.
Strengths: You respected your teammate's wishes and their drive to compete.
Weaknesses: Pushing through an injury often turns a short break into a long one. A leader who stays silent sets the team's standard.
Choice: Bench the runner in front of the whole team so everyone knows you're serious about safety.
Outcome: The runner is embarrassed and angry, feels you betrayed their trust, and stops telling you things. Another team member starts hiding a sore knee.
Strengths: You stopped the injury from getting worse.
Weaknesses: Doing it publicly made people afraid to report pain — the opposite of what you want.
Choice: Talk with the runner privately, tell them you need to let the instructor know, and work out a plan: rest, get it checked, and stay part of the team in a way that doesn't hurt the injury.
Outcome: The runner is frustrated but agrees. The instructor has them see the school trainer, who catches it early. With rest and lighter training they're back in time for the competition, at a slightly lower pace. The team learns that reporting pain is what good teammates do.
Strengths: You protected your teammate's health and trust, and kept the team honest.
Weaknesses: The team trains without its best runner for a while, and the result is uncertain.
Principles:
- Progression means challenging the body without overtraining — and recovery is part of training.
- Leaders look out for the people in their care; accountability includes what you fail to do.
- How you handle one problem teaches the whole team what's expected.
Other approaches:
Before a hard training block, tell the whole team up front: report pain early and nobody will be judged for it.
Reflection: Have you ever hidden pain or a problem so you wouldn't let people down? What would have made it easier to speak up?
