Title: Tough feedback
Competency: Accepting criticism
Situation:
After drill practice, your instructor tells you in front of two other Cadets that your commands were too quiet and your squad was out of step because of it. You worked hard on your commands and you feel embarrassed.
Choice: Explain that the squad wasn't listening, not that your commands were quiet.
Outcome: The instructor says they'll watch again next week. The squad hears you blamed them. Nothing about your commands changes.
Strengths: You spoke up for yourself.
Weaknesses: Blaming others closes the door on getting better and costs you trust with your squad.
Choice: Say "Roger, I'll work on it," and ask what to practice.
Outcome: The instructor suggests practicing commands from the diaphragm and from the back of the gym. Next week your squad stays in step, and the instructor notices.
Strengths: You stayed calm, owned it, and turned criticism into a plan.
Weaknesses: It stings in the moment. You'll need to follow through, not just say it.
Choice: Say nothing, but stop volunteering to lead drill.
Outcome: You avoid the embarrassment, but you also stop growing. Someone else gets the next leadership chance.
Strengths: You avoided an argument.
Weaknesses: Quitting after criticism teaches you to avoid hard things — the opposite of leading.
Principles:
- Feedback is information, not an attack.
- Own your part first; you can raise other factors calmly later.
- Ask "What should I practice?" — it turns criticism into a plan.
Other approaches:
Accept the feedback in the moment. If something about it felt unfair (like being corrected in front of others), ask the instructor for a quick word privately afterward — calmly, and after you've already worked on the feedback.
Reflection: What would you do differently next time? How do you usually react to criticism?
Notice: Feeling embarrassed is normal; what matters is what you do in the next ten seconds.
Discussion:
- Why is 'Roger, I'll work on it' hard to say when you feel embarrassed?
- How can you raise other factors (like a noisy squad) later without sounding like you're making excuses?
- What's the cost of quietly stopping volunteering?
Follow-up: Practice: each Cadet gives one drill command at full volume, and a partner gives one specific, kind piece of feedback.
