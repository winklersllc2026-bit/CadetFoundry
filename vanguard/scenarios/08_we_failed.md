Title: When the team falls short
Competency: Handling failure
Situation:
Your drill team trained for six weeks for a competition and placed last. On the bus home, Cadets are quiet. Some are blaming the Cadet who missed a turn in the routine. Everyone is looking at you, the team commander.
Choice: Tell the team the judges were unfair.
Outcome: The team feels better for the night. Next season, the same mistakes happen, because nobody looked at what went wrong.
Strengths: You protected morale in a hard moment.
Weaknesses: Blaming outside factors stops a team from learning.
Choice: Say you're proud of the effort, that you'll look at what to fix together next week, and that nobody gets blamed.
Outcome: The bus relaxes a little. At the after-action review, the team finds three things to fix, including the turn — and the Cadet who missed it volunteers to help lead practice.
Strengths: You owned the result as the leader, protected the Cadet being blamed, and turned the loss into a plan.
Weaknesses: It's hard to stay steady when you're disappointed too.
Choice: Agree that the Cadet who missed the turn cost you the competition.
Outcome: The Cadet quits the team. Others become afraid to make mistakes in practice, and the team gets more tense.
Strengths: You were honest about one mistake.
Weaknesses: A team rarely loses because of one person. Blaming one Cadet broke trust.
Principles:
- The leader owns the team's results.
- Review what happened and what to change — not who to blame.
- How you respond to failure teaches your team how to handle it.
Other approaches:
Hold a short after-action review: What was supposed to happen? What happened? Why? What will we do differently? Let the team find the answers.
Reflection: What would you do differently next time? What did you learn from a time you fell short?
